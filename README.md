## Project Overview

This research analyzes digital distraction during online learning using screen-time data collected from 214 undergraduate students over 14 weeks.

The study examines:

* Off-task activities during online lectures
* Distraction patterns across courses and timings
* Relationship between screen-time behavior and academic performance

---

## Tools & Technologies

* ActivityWatch
* Moodle LMS
* Zoom
* R 4.3.1
* ggplot2
* lme4

---

## Key Findings

* Students spent an average of 23.4 minutes per 90-minute lecture on off-task activities.
* Social media and messaging apps caused most distractions.
* Higher off-task behavior was linked to lower academic performance.
* Distraction increased during the beginning and ending parts of lectures.

---

## Research Contribution

* Introduced a three-tier application classification system:

  * On-task
  * Ambiguous
  * Off-task
* Used passive screen-time tracking instead of self-reported surveys.
* Provided insights for improving online learning systems.
